# Video: select before decorating

Inspect supplied footage before deciding the story. Record useful moments by source time and what actually occurs. A filename or attractive thumbnail does not establish an action, location or chronology.

Find the promise appropriate to the format. A travel short may open on an intriguing result; a tutorial may need context and a safe step order. Preserve the user's requested scope: a subtitle-only fix does not authorize restructuring footage.

Build a rough sequence from meaningful changes: orientation, action, detail and resolution as the material supports. Each cut should add information, emotion or useful rhythm. Avoid both uniform shot lengths by reflex and rapid cuts merely to manufacture energy.

Choose framing around the actual action. For vertical crops, follow the working area rather than always center-cropping. Do not sacrifice the cup, hand, tool or result that makes the shot understandable. A slowed ending should land on the observed result, not a nearby unfinished action.

Compose sound with the edit. Speech leads when it carries the message; ambience, music and effects can support continuity or articulate a change. Beats are one possible edit reference, not a requirement for every cut. Avoid layering music and effects that compete with narration.

Subtitles need readable grouping, safe placement and enough hold. Maintain meaning and source facts. Do not label stock footage as a real venue or invent reactions, reviews or product claims. Credit and license the source media separately from your code.

## Inspect the export

Watch the complete export with sound where possible, including the first/last seconds and every cut. Check continuity, crop, caption timing, audio balance, frame/timebase, duration and decode errors. A technically valid MP4 can still have a weak story. A selected frame cannot prove continuity.

Fix the highest-impact editorial mismatch first. Keep source-to-edit decisions reproducible. If only frame sampling/metadata was available, document those observations and leave full playback/listening unconfirmed. Publishing is a separate action governed by the user's request.
