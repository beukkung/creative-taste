# Music: a reason for every entrance

Use the available composition/DAW tools. Original synthesis is a valid instrument choice, not evidence that its sound has been heard.

Define the musical job first: foreground identity, support for speech, atmosphere, movement or transition. Map the duration into an opening gesture, development and ending; short work still needs a deliberate landing.

Build a recognizable motif from a small interval/rhythm/timbre idea. Develop it by changing a meaningful property while retaining identity. Extra notes are not the only form of development: register, voicing, silence, articulation and harmonic context can do more.

Arrange an attention hierarchy. Avoid making every instrument attack at once by default. Let timbre, register, envelope and stereo position distribute roles. Use harmonic motion and voice leading to make emotion specific; a genre name alone does not specify a composition.

For narration, inspect the actual speech timing. Leave room for consonants and phrase starts; compare with and without speech. Consider midrange overlap, transient density and automation before simply lowering the entire track. Ducking cannot rescue an arrangement whose lead competes with every spoken phrase. Preserve the supplied voice unless editing it is requested.

An ending needs musical closure and enough tail for the chosen material. A fade can serve the brief, but should not disguise an unfinished phrase. Avoid a waveform discontinuity at the export boundary.

## Verify and listen

Render the requested duration/sample format. Check finite samples, clipping, true peak, loudness, silence, mono compatibility and boundaries as relevant. Listen to the complete export and the intended mix when a listening route exists; compare at matched playback loudness. Note masking, harshness, repetitive fatigue and the emotional arc.

If listening is unavailable, report measured properties and provide playable audio. Do not turn a loudness number or waveform into a subjective quality verdict. Preserve instrument/score source and provenance; do not claim synthesis is a recording of a real instrument or imitate a named artist when the brief requests originality.
