# Motion graphics: compose in time

Use the production renderer/editor already appropriate to the task. The still image and the movement must support the same message.

Give each beat a job: establish, change, hold or resolve. Read the timeline as overlapping attention demands. Important text needs an actual readable hold after its entrance, not just total time somewhere on screen. Test with a first-time viewer's reading pace and the real language.

Choose a motion vocabulary from the subject's behavior. Translation, scale, deformation, reveal or camera movement imply different things. Easing should express mass and intent, not a universal curve rule. Overshoot may suit playful material; it can undermine a precise instrument display. Settle sufficiently before introducing a competing event.

Use anticipation and follow-through when they clarify cause and effect. Delay supporting elements to establish hierarchy, rather than staggering everything mechanically. Transitions can carry an element's identity into the next composition; not every change needs a new effect.

Check spatial endpoints before polishing curves. Typography, alignment and negative space must work during the hold. In Thai, use a shaping-aware renderer and inspect marks at export resolution. Protect safe areas and transparent-output requirements.

## Inspect the temporal result

Render the exact aspect ratio, frame rate and duration. Inspect start/end frames and the busiest overlaps, then play the complete export when possible. Check sudden cuts, unreadable holds, unintended blank frames, clipping, alpha edges and loop seams where relevant. A contact sheet verifies selected states, not the intervening movement.

For web motion, include reduced-motion behavior that preserves the content. For a rendered clip, honor any brief-specific motion/accessibility alternative. If playback is not available, provide the actual video and name the sampled timepoints instead of claiming full motion review.
