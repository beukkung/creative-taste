# Baseline observations recorded before skill authoring

All four baseline exports were completed before creative-taste was written. They were produced in independent worker contexts without the new skill, using normal available tools and any existing skills workers selected. None was deliberately degraded.

- Frontend: coherent lavender/ink direction, clear Thai hierarchy and responsive/keyboard proof. One actual defect found by its worker was image height/aspect ratio handling; it was repaired before freezing. Enlarged low-resolution source images remain soft. A useful new skill should not prohibit this already coherent aesthetic merely to force a different look.
- Music: original six-bar D-major bed at 72 BPM, deliberate final tonic and speech ducking. Twenty seconds, no clipped samples; actual listening unavailable. The skill should separate objective verification from an unsupported claim of superior sound.
- Motion: shaped Thai phrases, visible reading holds and a restrained cup-rim motif. Actual video exists; metadata and frame samples alone do not prove all temporal behavior was watched.
- Video: the worker selected footage by inspecting its real content and repaired an ending that initially stopped on bloom instead of the finished cup. This supports making source-to-story alignment explicit, without prescribing every edit as a hook montage.

These observations identify responsibilities for the shared skill: preserve intentional choices, match attention to the brief, trace reference evidence, inspect the exported medium, and name unverified perceptual claims. They do not establish that the baseline lacks taste or that the after condition will win.

The temporal baseline worker handled motion and video in one fresh context and reported a shared-session time lower bound, not individual measured production times. Narration became available during the baseline music run. Both are retained as experimental limitations.
